Preliminary data-efficiency curve under a common training recipe¶

Stage 5 scientific report. This notebook asks how validation performance changes from 25 to 100 to 500 labelled training images when architecture, initialization rules and optimization are held fixed. The comparison uses one nested Seed 17 subset family and the same northern validation region. It is an exploratory learning curve, not a final label requirement or test result.

1. Research question¶

At 100 labelled images, ImageNet encoder initialization outperformed random initialization on two training-image selections. We now ask two linked questions: does performance improve when the labelled set grows, and does the pretraining advantage change with data amount?

The primary metric is mean per-image roof intersection over union (IoU) at probability threshold 0.5. IoU measures predicted–reference overlap divided by their union. Dice and average precision (AP) are secondary metrics. All model selection uses the same 289-image validation region; the geographically held-out test region remains locked.

2. Fixed comparison and unequal image exposure¶

The main curve contains exactly six Seed 17 runs: 25, 100 and 500 training images crossed with random and ImageNet initialization. The saved subsets are nested, so every smaller subset is contained in the next larger one. Every run uses 4,000 optimizer updates, learning rate 1e-3 through step 2,000 and 1e-4 thereafter, batch size 4, D4 augmentation and the same seeds for model construction, data order and augmentation.

Equal update counts do not imply equal exposure. The retained final incomplete batch means n=25 processes fewer total examples but repeats each image much more often. “Data passages” below are processed examples divided by labelled images; they are an exposure description rather than independent observations.

5. Training trajectories and late behavior¶

The upper two rows show validation IoU and validation loss under deterministic evaluation. The lower row shows the augmented training-mode objective averaged over the previous 100 updates. It helps diagnose optimization but is not directly comparable to validation loss because preprocessing and model mode differ. The vertical line marks the learning-rate drop.

display(Image(filename=ROOT / 'reports/figures/data_efficiency_histories.png'))

At n=25, both models fit the small training subset very closely while validation loss rises late. ImageNet selects step 1,700 and loses 0.009 IoU by step 4,000; random selects step 2,000 and then remains roughly flat. This combination of low training loss, large train–validation gaps and worsening late validation loss is evidence of overfitting under the common budget.

At n=500, both models improve after step 2,000. Random reaches its best at step 3,200 and has a strong positive late IoU trend; ImageNet reaches its best at step 3,700. Their endpoints remain close to their maxima. The common 4,000-update budget therefore does not demonstrate equal convergence across sizes and may still limit the larger subset.

7. Limitations and next decision¶

This preliminary curve uses one nested subset repetition and one shared validation region. The sample-size points are dependent because the subsets are nested, and validation reuse introduces selection bias across the preceding optimization work. Equal updates expose n=25 to far more repeated views than n=500. The results do not establish statistical significance, performance in another city, an optimal horizon for every size or a final required label count. No test image or test prediction was used.

The most direct next limited block is to repeat n=25 and n=500 on the saved Seed 29 nested subsets with the same recipe and seeds (four runs). Together with the completed n=100 Seed 29 pair, this would test whether the curve shape, early small-set overfitting and pretraining advantage depend strongly on training-image selection before changing weight decay, BatchNorm, augmentation or the loss. This is a recommendation only; no additional training is started here.
